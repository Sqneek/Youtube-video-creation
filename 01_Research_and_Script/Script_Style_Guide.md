# Script Instructions

## Voice
- Talk like a smart, well-read friend telling you something wild they just learned — not a documentary narrator, not a lecture, not trailer copy.
- Contractions throughout, wherever a real person would use them.
- Second person allowed at key moments.
- Vary sentence length on purpose. A longer sentence with a couple of clauses, then a short blunt one. Perfectly even, symmetrical sentences back-to-back is the single biggest tell that it's not a real person talking — break the rhythm on purpose.
- One or two small personal-reaction beats per script are fine and good ("which, honestly, is a wild thing for a species to survive" / "and yeah, it's as bad as it sounds") — sparingly, not a tic repeated every paragraph.
- Define jargon inline, same sentence.
- Confident claims only; flag disputed facts as disputed — in plain language ("nobody's actually proven that" not "it should be noted that this claim remains unsubstantiated").

## Structure (fixed order, length scales with video length)
1. Hook (15–30s) — question/fact/tension, not exposition
2. Context/Setup — who/where/when/why, concrete detail
3. Body — 3–6 beats, each with a re-hook transition, explicit cause-and-effect
4. Turning point/climax — most narrative weight
5. Resolution/Aftermath — ties back to hook's stakes
6. Takeaway/Close — larger pattern or parallel, quotable closing line

## Visual traceability
- Write each beat as a concrete, on-screen action, not an abstract statement
- Give recurring locations/people/objects one visual detail on first mention
- Keep each image-anchoring sentence quotable on its own
- Flag any beat needing a distinct visual style (legend, flashback, modern-era) inline

## Avoid
- Melodrama, over-the-top language
- Long exposition blocks with no throughline
- Disclaimers/apologies/"hey guys" openers
- Passive-voice textbook phrasing
- Stock AI-narrator crutches — use at most once per whole script, and only if nothing plainer works:
  - "Here's the thing" / "Here's the twist" / "Here's the catch" / "Here's what actually happened"
  - "Imagine [X]" as an opener
  - "It gets stranger" / "it gets weirder" as a transition
  - Rule-of-three lists where every item starts with the same word ("Not just X. Not just Y. But Z.")
  - Tidy mirrored parallelism ("It's not a story about X. It's a story about Y.")
- A whole script where every paragraph is the same clean shape — real speech has some uneven, run-on, or blunt one-line sentences mixed in.

## Human tells (use lightly — a few per script, not every beat)
- A parenthetical dropped mid-sentence: "(which, if you think about it, is insane)"
- A trailing thought or self-correction: "Or — well, that's the story people tell."
- A blunt short sentence for emphasis: "It didn't work. At all."
- A plain, first-person-adjacent reaction instead of a neutral observation: "That's genuinely disgusting, and also kind of brilliant."

## Checklist
- [ ] Hook lands in 30s
- [ ] Jargon defined on first use
- [ ] Each beat re-hooks
- [ ] Cause-and-effect explicit
- [ ] Disputed facts flagged
- [ ] Close ties to hook, ends on clear line
- [ ] Recurring location/person/object has visual detail on first mention
- [ ] No more than one "here's the [twist/catch/thing]" framing device in the whole script
- [ ] Sentence rhythm varies — not three same-length sentences in a row
- [ ] Read aloud: does this sound like a person telling a friend, or a documentary narrating?
